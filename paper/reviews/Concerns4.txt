Remaining concerns
1. The session proxy is still uncalibrated, and the conclusion depends on the granularity.
The gap goes from 0.13/0.19 (random) to about 0 (~140 flows per cluster), so the verdict hinges on which granularity is session-like. The paper says the three settings “bracket” the unknown structure but never estimates it. A cheap estimate would be the number of source captures versus the number of flows: the average flows per capture file would indicate which setting is relevant. If it is nearer ~100+, the coarsest setting is the meaningful one. Please state this, even roughly.
Two related points:
At the coarsest setting, half-model macro F1 is only 0.53–0.61 and RF Δ has SD 0.111, so "Δ ≈ 0" there is weak evidence.
Some sentences overcorrect. “They should not be read as an effect of the VPN” is stronger than the evidence, since the RF gap is still positive in all 11 repeats at ~35 flows. The abstract’s “cannot be attributed to tunnelling” is the better wording, so use it throughout.
2. The min-IAT “credit redistribution” conclusion holds for XGBoost but not clearly for RF.
The text says a substantial part of the visible SHAP shift is Shapley credit moving among correlated timing features. If so, the combined share of the three minima should be roughly stable across contexts. From Table V (and the unchanged RF min_fiat value of 4.35 for Non-VPN):
So for XGBoost the redistribution story fits, but for RF the block itself gains about 6 points. Please report block-summed SHAP shares in Table IX and temper the sentence for RF.
Also, permutation importance is not a neutral arbiter with correlated features (ref. [34]). Say so, or add a grouped or conditional permutation check.
3. The feature-list transfer should be read as a 2×2 (list × target), not “own vs transferred”.
For RF, the VPN-derived list does better on both targets:
k = 10: +0.004 on the Non-VPN target and +0.013 on the VPN target.
k = 5: +0.026 on the Non-VPN target and +0.046 on the VPN target.
So the “cost of transfer” at the VPN target mostly reflects that the Non-VPN list is a worse list overall. The context-match effect (the sum of the two own-minus-transferred contrasts) is small:
Report this interaction term, and soften “a measurable cost” for the VPN RF target.
4. The XGBoost family-level gap does not shrink under grouping.
Table X shows XGB summed-family Δ at +0.075 (random) vs +0.077 (grouped ~10), and clusters at 0.099 vs 0.091. That sits awkwardly with the narrative that the gap mostly reflects related-flow redundancy. It needs a sentence of discussion, and the abstract’s “gap shrinks substantially” should be scoped to feature level.
5. Model-transfer comparison is loose.
Section VIII-A compares transfer F1 (0.43–0.61) with cluster-CV F1 (0.47–0.64) to say the contexts differ “at least as much” as distant clusters. These protocols differ (50 clusters, 80% training data, versus a full other-context model), and class mix and sessions are confounded in the transfer. Hedge it.
